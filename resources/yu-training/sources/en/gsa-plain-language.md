## Adopted teaching abstractions

1. Name the intended reader before revising; clarity depends on what that reader already knows and needs.
2. Use a “who does what?” check when a sentence hides responsibility. Active voice is a clarity option, not a universal literary rule.
3. Prefer a direct verb when a noun-heavy expression makes an action difficult to see.
4. Ask another reader to paraphrase a short passage. Compare the paraphrase with the creator's intention, then let the creator choose the repair.
5. Test, revise, and test again on a small passage rather than waiting until the entire work is finished.

## Excluded

- Government-specific legal and administrative examples.
- Any claim that short sentences, present tense, or active voice are always artistically superior.
- Automatic simplification that removes a creator's voice, rhythm, ambiguity, or intentional style.
