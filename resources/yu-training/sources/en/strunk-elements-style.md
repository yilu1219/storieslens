## Adopted teaching abstractions

1. Check whether each sentence is complete before improving its style.
2. Keep an opening modifier attached to the person or thing it actually describes.
3. Keep words that belong together close enough that a reader cannot attach them to the wrong idea.
4. When a list or comparison presents parallel ideas, make their grammatical pattern easy to follow.
5. Give one paragraph one readable center, while allowing dialogue, transitions, and deliberate literary fragments to work differently.
6. Keep tense and point of view consistent unless the creator intentionally changes them.
7. Treat punctuation as a signal of sentence relationships, not decoration.
